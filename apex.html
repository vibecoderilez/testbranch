Custom model development

Rent a capability, or own one.

Every company using a frontier API is renting the same intelligence as its competitors, metered by the token, with its data crossing someone else's boundary. Fine-tuning an open-weight model on your own processes produces something none of them have and none of them can buy. For a company at your size, that is the most durable technical advantage available right now.

01

Extract

We mine your real artifacts — resolved tickets, existing scripts, policy docs, historical decisions, correspondence — into a structured training corpus. Your institutional knowledge becomes a dataset.

02

Train

QLoRA fine-tuning against an open-weight base on rented GPU capacity. No fixed infrastructure cost, no long-term hardware commitment, checkpointed so a failed node costs an hour instead of a week.

03

Evaluate

Scored against a held-out set of your own cases before anything ships. We measure against the frontier model it is replacing, per task type, so the tradeoff is a number rather than a hunch.

04

Deploy

Quantized to run on a single GPU. Your VPC, a box in your building, or a managed endpoint — whichever your compliance posture requires. Bound directly to the agent runtime.

05

Compound

Every corrected output feeds the next training set. The model gets measurably better at your specific work each quarter, and the asset appreciates instead of depreciating.

Sized for real budgets

Custom models used to mean a research team and a seven-figure cluster. That is no longer true. A quantized fine-tune runs on a single consumer-class GPU, and training happens on rented capacity billed by the hour. Small enterprises can now hold a genuine model asset, and most of them do not know it yet.

Where your data never goes

Regulated records, customer PII, contract terms, proprietary pricing. A model running inside your perimeter means those never leave it — not for inference, not for logging, not for a vendor's future training run. For a lot of buyers this is the only architecture legal will approve.

You own the weights

Not a tenant on someone else's fine-tune, not a license that lapses. The artifact is yours, it transfers if you sell the company, and it keeps working if we stop existing.

Routed, not replaced

This is the part people get wrong. A custom model does not replace the frontier model; it absorbs the high-volume, well-shaped work where a specialist beats a generalist on both cost and latency. Hard reasoning still escalates. The routing layer decides per request, and the cost curve flattens without the quality dropping.

Ongoing
Controls

Built to survive a conversation with your auditor.

Finance automation has a higher bar than marketing automation. These aren't add-ons we sell separately; they're how every system ships.

Approval gates

Humans on the consequential steps

Anything that posts to the ledger, moves money, or leaves your organization stops for a named approver. The agent prepares; a person commits.

Evaluation

Regression suites for agents

Each agent has a golden dataset of past cases. Every prompt or model change is scored against it before it ships. Drift is caught by a test, not by a controller in March.

Observability

Full trace on every action

What the agent saw, what it decided, which tool it called, what came back. Exportable, timestamped, and tied to the run that produced the entry.

Access

Least privilege by default

Each agent gets its own credential set with only the tools its job requires. Revoking one agent doesn't take the others down.

Segregation of duties

Preserved, not bypassed

Agent identities are modeled inside your existing SoD matrix. An agent that prepares cannot be the one that approves.

Exit

No lock-in by design

MCP servers, prompts, evals, and model weights are yours and documented. If you bring it in-house or hire someone else, everything transfers.
